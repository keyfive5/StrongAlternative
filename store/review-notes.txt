Thank you for the review of 1.0.0. We took the 4.3 feedback seriously: on a fresh install the coach had no history to work from, so the app looked like any other log. This build changes what the app does, not just how it looks.

WHAT IS NEW
- Readiness: after the first hard set, the app compares it with the lifter's last three sessions. On an off day it sizes the remaining sets to today's strength; on a strong day it suggests more. One tap applies it.
- Plateau diagnosis: when a lift stalls, the app reads the log (weekly sets for that muscle against the user's target, how often the lift is trained, working-set count, rep range) and lists what to change, plus a variation of the same movement to rotate to.
- Starting estimates: a lift never done before gets a starting weight estimated from the user's strength on a related lift.
- A training goal (strength / muscle / both) that sets every default rep range.
- A first-launch walkthrough with "Explore with sample data".

HOW TO SEE IT IN 30 SECONDS
1. On first launch tap Continue twice, then "Explore with sample data" (six months of example training, removable in one tap).
2. Train tab > Push. Each exercise shows today's target. Type 80 kg and 2 reps in the first bench set and tick it: the off-day coach appears with a "Use" button.
3. Exercises tab > Overhead Press (Barbell): "Why it has stalled".
4. In a workout, Add exercises > Incline Bench Press (Barbell): "Start around 62.5 kg", estimated from the bench press.
No account, no purchase. The app makes no network requests.

ANSWERS TO YOUR QUESTIONS
1. It tells a lifter what weight and reps to do on every set, from their own history, and adjusts for off days and stalls. The problem: most lifters guess loads, stall without knowing why, and lose track of what they did last time.
2. People who lift weights on a structured plan, beginner to intermediate, who want progress without paying a coach. The developer is one: he logged his training in another app and wanted progression guidance that is not behind a subscription.
3. Existing logs record what you did. Overload prescribes what to do next: live off-day adjustment, a plateau diagnosis from the user's own log, and starting estimates across related lifts, free and fully on-device. It also imports history from other apps so the coach works from day one.
4. There was no external beta for 1.0.0. Testing was internal: 338 automated tests covering the coaching logic, one-rep-max maths, records, plate maths, CSV import from other apps' export formats, and crash-safe saving; plus walking every screen with six months of simulated training. Internal review before submission found and fixed an on-device save race and forms hidden by the keyboard. We would welcome a TestFlight-based review if helpful.
5. Standalone. The account's other apps are unrelated utilities (notes, QR codes, currency, timers, dice/coin).
6. No. No other app on the account logs training or coaches lifting. The closest, Boxing Rounds, is a round timer with no history or logging.
7. No shared frameworks. Two small utilities (an SVG icon component and a file-storage adapter) follow patterns from the same developer's earlier app; all training, coaching and import logic is unique to this app. Build and upload scripts are shared but are not in the binary.
8. No. Only standard open-source frameworks (React Native, Expo, react-native-svg).
9. No. It is the developer's own product and concept, not made for a client, not from a template or app-generation service.
